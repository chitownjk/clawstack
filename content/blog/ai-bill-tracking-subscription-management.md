The average American household spends over $200 per month on subscriptions. Most people cannot name half of them from memory. The ones you forgot about are the ones costing you the most, because you are paying for something you are not using.

The fix is not another budgeting app. It is AI that reads your email and finds every subscription and bill automatically.

## The subscription creep problem

Subscription creep happens slowly. You sign up for a free trial and forget to cancel. A price increase goes unnoticed because the charge is on auto-pay. You keep paying for a service you stopped using six months ago.

The reason this happens is not irresponsibility. It is information architecture. Subscription confirmations, renewal notices, and price change emails all arrive in your inbox -- and promptly get buried under everything else. The information exists. It is just not surfaced in a useful way.

## Email as financial intelligence

Every subscription you pay for generates email. Sign-up confirmations, monthly receipts, annual renewal notices, price change notifications, and payment failure alerts. Your inbox is a complete record of your financial commitments, just scattered across thousands of messages.

AI email scanning extracts this information systematically. It reads your receipt emails and identifies the service, amount, billing frequency, and next due date. It reads renewal notices and flags upcoming charges. It reads price change emails and alerts you before the new rate kicks in.

The result is a complete subscription inventory that you did not have to build manually.

## From tracking to action

Knowing what you pay for is step one. Step two is doing something about it.

A good AI bill tracker does not just list your subscriptions. It groups them by category so you can see how much you spend on streaming, how much on productivity tools, how much on services you have not logged into in months. It flags subscriptions with upcoming renewals so you can decide whether to keep them. It tracks price increases so you know when a $9.99 plan quietly became $14.99.

Some people discover they are paying for three different cloud storage services when they only need one. Others find trials they forgot to cancel months ago. The average savings from a subscription audit is $30-50 per month -- and that is recurring savings.

## Why not use Mint or a budgeting app?

Traditional budgeting apps work from bank transaction data. They see charges but often cannot tell you what the charge is for beyond "GOOG*YOUTUBE $13.99." They also require connecting your bank, which is a bigger trust decision than connecting your email.

Email-based tracking has richer context. The receipt email tells you exactly what the subscription is, when it renews, and what plan you are on. No bank connection required.

The two approaches are complementary. Bank-based tracking catches charges that do not generate email. Email-based tracking gives you context that bank data lacks.

## How Tiker handles bills

Tiker scans your inbox for bills, receipts, and subscription confirmations as part of its email intelligence feature. It classifies each one, extracts the key details, and adds them to your financial overview.

You get a dashboard showing your total monthly subscriptions, upcoming bills, and services you have not used recently. It surfaces this in your daily briefing when something needs attention -- like a trial ending tomorrow or a bill that is overdue.

No separate app to install, no bank account to connect, no spreadsheet to maintain. Just intelligence extracted from email you were already receiving.
