You have a meeting in 10 minutes. Who is on the call? What did you talk about last time? Is there anything you should bring up? If you are scrambling to remember, you are not alone.

Most people walk into meetings underprepared, not because they do not care, but because prep takes time they do not have. AI meeting prep changes that equation by doing the research for you automatically.

## What AI meeting prep covers

Good meeting prep answers three questions: who am I meeting, what is the context, and what should I talk about?

For the "who" question, AI looks up each attendee. Their role, their company, their recent activity. If you have interacted with them before, it pulls that history. If you have not, it gives you enough background to have a real conversation instead of awkward small talk.

For the "context" question, AI searches your tasks, past activities, and email threads for anything related to this meeting or these people. If you sent them a proposal last month, it surfaces that. If there is an open task related to their company, it connects the dots.

For the "what to talk about" question, AI generates suggested talking points and questions based on the context it found. These are not generic suggestions. They are specific to the meeting, the attendees, and your history with them.

## The 10-minute advantage

The difference between walking into a meeting cold and walking in with a one-page prep doc is enormous. You remember names. You reference previous conversations. You ask informed questions. The other person thinks you are incredibly organized. In reality, your AI did the work while you were brushing your teeth.

For business meetings, this is even more valuable. Knowing a prospect's recent company news, their LinkedIn activity, or their open support tickets gives you a real advantage. Not in a manipulative way -- in a "I actually care about your situation" way.

## How it works technically

A meeting prep engine pulls data from multiple sources. Your calendar provides the event details and attendee list. Email and task history provide prior context. LinkedIn or company databases provide background on attendees. AI synthesizes all of this into a structured brief.

The synthesis step is where the value really lives. Raw data from five sources is not useful. A concise brief that says "Sarah has been at Acme Corp for 3 years, you last spoke about the Q2 roadmap, and she emailed you a doc to review before this call" -- that is useful.

## When to automate it

Not every meeting needs AI prep. Your weekly team standup does not need attendee research. But any meeting with external people, any meeting where deals or decisions are on the table, and any meeting where you have not spoken to the attendees recently -- those all benefit from automated prep.

The best approach is to automate everything and let the AI decide what is worth surfacing. If there is nothing interesting to report, the prep brief is short. If there is a lot of context, you get a detailed brief. Either way, it took zero effort on your part.
