Every time you connect an AI tool to your Gmail, you are handing a company access to your most personal data. Your flight plans, your medical appointments, your financial statements, your private conversations. Most people do this without a second thought because the convenience is worth it.

But there is an alternative: running the AI on your own infrastructure.

## The trust problem

Cloud AI tools require trust at two levels. First, you trust that the company handles your data responsibly today. Second, you trust that they will continue to do so in the future, through acquisitions, policy changes, data breaches, and business model pivots.

History suggests this trust is misplaced. Companies get acquired and data policies change. Startups run out of money and sell user data as an asset. Even well-intentioned companies have breaches. When the data in question is your complete email history and calendar, the stakes are high.

## What self-hosting gives you

Self-hosted means the software runs on your own server. Your data never leaves your infrastructure. There is no cloud account, no third-party storage, no data sharing agreements.

For an AI tool that reads your email and calendar, this is meaningful. Your flight itineraries, bill amounts, meeting attendees, and personal tasks stay on hardware you control. No startup pivot, acquisition, or policy change can affect your data.

The tradeoff is real: you need to set up and maintain a server. For technical users, this is an afternoon of work. For non-technical users, it is a barrier. But the option should exist.

## Open source as verification

Self-hosting only works if you can verify what the software actually does. If the code is proprietary, you are trusting the developer just as much as you would trust a cloud service.

Open source changes that equation. When the code is public, anyone can audit it. You can see exactly how your email is processed, what data is stored, and whether anything is phoned home. The community can catch issues that a single company might miss or hide.

This is why Tiker is MIT-licensed and fully open source. The same code that runs on our cloud service is available for you to run yourself. Nothing hidden, nothing proprietary.

## The hybrid approach

You do not have to choose between cloud convenience and self-hosted control. Many self-hosted tools, Tiker included, offer both options.

Start with the cloud version. It is faster to set up and requires zero maintenance. If you decide you want more control, export your data and switch to self-hosted. The transition is seamless because it is the same software.

For most people, the cloud version with strong encryption is sufficient. For people handling sensitive data -- financial advisors, lawyers, medical professionals, or anyone who takes privacy seriously -- self-hosting removes the trust requirement entirely.

## The ecosystem is growing

Self-hosted AI tools used to mean "run a basic model on your laptop." The ecosystem has matured significantly. You can now self-host full-featured AI applications that rival or exceed their cloud counterparts.

The tools exist. The documentation is better. The deployment story (Docker, one-click installs) has simplified dramatically. If you have been waiting for self-hosted AI to become practical, it has.

Your data is yours. Your AI should be too.
